# Project Vision

nattapong.dev is a living, long-term personal engineering website. It should grow alongside Nattapong's work, knowledge, and professional development while remaining understandable, maintainable, and relevant over many years.

The website should become the central record of what Nattapong builds, studies, learns, and understands. It should represent engineering through real work, evidence, and reflection rather than through screenshots, technology lists, or promotional claims alone.

The project succeeds by combining quick understanding with meaningful depth. Visitors should be able to form an accurate first impression without reading everything, while technical readers should be able to inspect the decisions and evidence behind the work.

# Website Identity

nattapong.dev is a personal engineering website, not merely a portfolio. Portfolio and hiring value are useful functions of the website, but they do not define its entire identity.

The website is a cumulative record of engineering projects, research, technical notes, experiments, learning progression, open-source work, and personal and professional development. It should show both outcomes and the processes that produced them: questions, constraints, decisions, changes in understanding, and unfinished paths.

Its character should be calm, precise, thoughtful, technically credible, and recognizably personal. It should feel intentionally authored by an engineer rather than assembled from generic marketing language, fashionable effects, or copied reference aesthetics.

# Purpose

The website should help visitors understand:

- Who Nattapong is in a relevant personal and professional context.
- What he builds, studies, documents, and learns.
- How he approaches problems and makes engineering decisions.
- What evidence supports the work and what its current status is.
- How his knowledge and capabilities develop over time.
- Where to find deeper technical material, related work, source material when available, and current contact information.

It should also serve Nattapong as a durable knowledge and development record. Publishing should make it possible to connect projects with research, notes, experiments, case studies, and later reflections without rewriting earlier work into a falsely polished narrative.

# Core Principles

- **Engineering first:** Emphasize problems, systems, reasoning, evidence, and learning rather than marketing presentation.
- **Clarity before decoration:** Use hierarchy, writing, and intentional visual design to direct attention. Visual elements must support understanding.
- **Progressive disclosure:** Begin with concise, plain-language context and allow interested readers to continue into deeper technical explanation.
- **Readability:** Make scanning and sustained reading comfortable. Technical density should appear where it is useful, not across every part of the experience.
- **Honesty:** Distinguish facts, measurements, observations, expectations, hypotheses, and personal reflection. Do not imply more than the available evidence supports.
- **Timelessness:** Prefer durable principles and content-led choices over trends that would require frequent redesign.
- **Responsive meaning:** Preserve the purpose and legibility of content across screen sizes and input methods rather than merely shrinking a desktop composition.
- **Maintainability:** Make new content and evolving work possible without repeated structural redesign or inconsistent one-off solutions.
- **Scalability:** Allow the body of work and its relationships to grow while keeping navigation, hierarchy, and content discovery understandable.
- **Evidence-driven decisions:** Resolve later choices about structure, visuals, interaction, and technology through real content, prototypes, constraints, and testing.

# What the Website Should Represent

- **Engineering projects:** Applied work explained through its problem, context, contribution, system, decisions, and result.
- **Research:** Questions, methods, evidence, findings, uncertainty, limitations, and current state.
- **Technical notes:** Useful explanations, implementation discoveries, and reusable understanding.
- **Experiments:** Focused attempts to test an idea, behavior, or technical possibility, including what happened and what remains unknown.
- **Learning progression:** Meaningful changes in understanding, milestones, mistakes, and reflections rather than an unfiltered activity stream.
- **Open-source work:** Contributions and repositories presented with accurate ownership, context, and status.
- **Engineering decisions:** Requirements, alternatives, constraints, tradeoffs, and reasoning that reveal how problems were approached.
- **Unfinished and evolving work:** Prototypes, work in progress, abandoned directions, and open questions identified honestly rather than presented as finished systems.
- **Personal and professional development:** Relevant background, experience, interests, and growth that help visitors understand the engineer behind the work.

These areas should reinforce one another. A project may produce research, notes, experiments, or a case study; later learning may revisit an earlier decision. The website should preserve those relationships without forcing every item into the same format.

# Audience

- **Recruiters, hiring teams, and technical interviewers:** They need a fast, accurate understanding of Nattapong's engineering direction, capabilities, selected work, professional context, and contact path, with deeper evidence available for evaluation.
- **Engineers and open-source readers:** They need enough technical substance to understand architecture, implementation choices, constraints, tests, tradeoffs, and results.
- **Lecturers and research collaborators:** They need clear research questions, methods, findings, limitations, uncertainty, and current status.
- **Students and general visitors:** They need approachable summaries and explanations that do not assume specialist knowledge, with optional paths into more detail.
- **Nattapong:** He needs a maintainable, trustworthy record that supports reflection, incremental publishing, related-content connections, and visible progression over time.

When audience needs differ, the website should use layered communication rather than reducing all content to either shallow promotion or specialist-only documentation.

# Content Scope

The content may include projects, case studies, research, technical articles, engineering and learning notes, experiments, open-source work, professional background, resume information, speaking material when it exists, and contact information.

Each item should have a clear purpose:

- Projects and case studies demonstrate applied engineering and decision-making.
- Research documents disciplined inquiry and the limits of current understanding.
- Technical writing and notes teach, explain, or preserve useful knowledge.
- Experiments document focused validation and exploration.
- Learning material records meaningful progression.
- Background and resume content provide concise professional context.
- Contact information makes appropriate follow-up straightforward.

The presence of a content type does not determine its page, navigation label, homepage position, or visual treatment. Those decisions should follow a content inventory and later design work. Technologies should appear in the context of real systems and decisions rather than as an isolated inventory of badges.

# Engineering Evidence

Engineering claims should be supported by relevant, understandable evidence. Depending on the work, this may include:

- **Architecture:** Components, responsibilities, boundaries, dependencies, and data or control flow.
- **Constraints:** Time, scope, resources, devices, data, compatibility, knowledge, or other limits that shaped the work.
- **Decisions:** The requirements or observations behind important choices, including meaningful alternatives.
- **Tradeoffs:** Both the benefits and costs of a chosen approach.
- **Tests:** What behavior was checked and what confidence the checks provide.
- **Results:** Verified outcomes, measurements, findings, or observations separated from expected benefits.
- **Limitations:** Known weaknesses, missing capabilities, uncertainty, and conditions under which conclusions apply.
- **Artifacts:** Relevant interfaces, diagrams, code excerpts, terminal output, benchmarks, photographs, or source links when they help answer a reader's question.

Every work item should state its status accurately. Finished, ongoing, experimental, paused, and abandoned work must not be presented as equivalent. Prototypes must not be described as production systems. Claims about contribution, ownership, maturity, scale, reliability, collaboration, and impact must match the available evidence and name Nattapong's role clearly.

Evidence should be layered. A concise summary should establish the problem, contribution, state, and result for broad audiences; deeper material may then explain architecture, implementation, testing, decisions, and lessons. Technical artifacts should never be included merely to create the appearance of complexity.

# Personal Identity and Originality

Originality should come from Nattapong's real work, questions, decisions, interests, and way of explaining them. Writing should use a direct personal voice where ownership, uncertainty, reflection, or learning matters, while remaining precise and professional.

Project framing should explain why the work mattered, what Nattapong was responsible for, what changed during the process, and what remains imperfect. Generic claims about passion, innovation, expertise, or impact should be replaced with specific context and evidence.

Visual identity should emerge from the content and from recurring patterns found in the actual body of work. Reference websites may contribute principles, but their palettes, typography, compositions, illustrations, interaction signatures, and motifs should not be copied or combined. The result should not look like an AI-generated template with engineering content inserted into interchangeable sections.

# Accessibility and Performance

Accessibility and performance are non-negotiable qualities of the website and part of its engineering credibility.

- Use clear document structure, meaningful headings and landmarks, descriptive links, and appropriate control semantics.
- Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, touch interaction, zoom, text enlargement, and layout reflow.
- Maintain sufficient contrast and avoid encoding meaning through color, position, or motion alone.
- Provide useful text alternatives for screenshots, diagrams, audio, video, demonstrations, and other non-text evidence.
- Ensure essential content and navigation do not depend on hover, autoplay, precise pointer movement, animation, or successful script execution.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and preserve the meaning of any animated explanation in a static or simplified form.
- Keep text, diagrams, interfaces, code, and controls legible across screen sizes; detailed desktop artifacts should be reorganized or replaced rather than reduced below readable size.
- Treat images, media, fonts, scripts, and interactive demonstrations as performance costs that must justify their value.
- Prioritize fast access to identity, selected work, reading content, and contact on realistic devices and network conditions.
- Prevent loading and visual effects from delaying content, shifting layout, or blocking interaction.

Specific accessibility criteria and performance budgets should be established later using representative content, prototypes, and the chosen technical approach.

# Long-Term Growth

The website should support incremental publishing and expansion without requiring a complete redesign whenever content, skills, or interests change. New work should fit a coherent content model, while meaningful differences among projects, research, notes, and experiments remain visible.

The architecture should favor durable content, reusable structures, consistent metadata, and clear relationships among related material. Growth should not automatically produce complex navigation, crowded pages, or a uniform grid of generic items.

Search, syndication, filtering, additional publishing formats, analytics, community features, or other capabilities may be considered later. They are possibilities, not commitments. Each should be added only when real content, visitor needs, maintenance capacity, privacy considerations, accessibility, performance, and technical constraints provide sufficient evidence.

Decisions about the sitemap, navigation, homepage sequence, visual system, interaction behavior, and implementation technology belong to later stages and should remain revisable as evidence develops.

# Non-Goals

nattapong.dev is not primarily:

- A conventional portfolio centered on polished outcomes and hiring conversion.
- A SaaS landing page with product marketing, repeated conversion prompts, or implied customer scale.
- A generic gallery of project cards, screenshots, and technology badges.
- A creative-agency showcase or award-oriented animation experience.
- An AI-generated website template or a collection of interchangeable design patterns.
- A visual clone, mixture, or surface-level adaptation of the reference websites.
- A social-media feed, productivity score, or unfiltered record of activity.
- A place to present unsupported claims, decorative technical artifacts, or unfinished work as mature production work.

The vision does not choose a final sitemap, navigation structure, homepage sequence, color system, typography system, layout pattern, animation behavior, framework, library, or theme foundation. It defines the website's purpose and principles, leaving those decisions to later evidence-driven design and engineering work.

# Success Criteria

The website is successful when:

- Visitors can quickly understand what kind of engineering work Nattapong does, how he thinks, and where to explore further or make contact.
- Projects, research, notes, experiments, learning, open-source work, and professional context form a coherent personal engineering record rather than a disconnected gallery.
- Technical readers can inspect relevant architecture, constraints, decisions, tradeoffs, tests, results, limitations, and source material when available.
- Work status, contribution, maturity, scale, uncertainty, and impact are represented accurately.
- Concise summaries remain useful to general visitors while deeper content serves technical and research audiences.
- The writing and presentation feel personal, specific, trustworthy, and distinct from generic templates or copied reference styles.
- Content remains readable, accessible, responsive, and performant across realistic conditions.
- New material can be added and related to existing work without weakening clarity or requiring a complete redesign.

# Open Questions

The following decisions require evidence from content inventory, wireframes, prototypes, accessibility testing, performance testing, or technical constraints:

- Which existing work best represents Nattapong's current engineering direction?
- How much mature content exists in each category, and what distinctions among projects, case studies, research, experiments, and notes will remain useful over time?
- Which evidence is available for each work item, and which claims can be verified?
- What content should receive first-layer prominence for the different audiences?
- What sitemap and navigation depth does the real content require?
- What homepage structure, if any, best supports quick understanding and deeper exploration?
- What visual character, color foundation, typography, and layout system best support representative content without copying the references?
- Which interactions or demonstrations genuinely need motion, and how should their reduced-motion equivalents work?
- Which responsive transformations are required for real diagrams, interfaces, code, media, and long-form writing?
- What accessibility acceptance criteria and performance budgets are appropriate for the eventual implementation?
- Which optional features are justified by demonstrated visitor needs and sustainable maintenance?
